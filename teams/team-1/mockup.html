🔥 HeatSnitch

An AI watchdog for district heating utilities: it watches every building's heat substation data and flags suspect meters — turning stolen heat into recovered revenue, without raising prices for honest customers.

Problem

  • 3–7% of distributed heat is never billed — magnet tricks, illegal bypasses, meter tampering.
  • The utility covers the loss by raising prices for everyone.
  • Honest customers lose trust — and some start cheating too. Theft breeds theft.
  • Nobody can audit 100,000+ substations by hand: today it's spot checks and crossed fingers.
  • A public scandal about cheating factories makes it worse — angry customers, boycotts, board panic.

Solution

  • A small AI model watches flow/return temperatures and consumption curves from every substation.
  • It flags a suspect meter, never a person — the utility's control team decides.
  • Flat fee, no per-accusation reward: the incentive to accuse honestly is built into the pricing.

Security & critical infrastructure

  • "Burglar's oracle": empty-flat detection → data stays on-prem at the utility, per-unit data never exported, access logging.
  • Weaponized flags: landlord pressures tenant, or spoofed telemetry frames a neighbour → flags are indicators only; signed meter data + mandatory human audit with explainable evidence.
  • MITM / replay on old Modbus links: attacker replays "normal" data → signed telemetry, device attestation, and an alarm when data looks too clean.
  • The evil twist: a leaked dashboard shows attackers which buildings are easy targets → no raw model scores exposed, rate-limited queries, query auditing.
  • Critical infrastructure (KRITIS): a state actor pivots from remote read-access into grid OT, or floods crews with mass-spoofed alerts during a January cold snap.
  • Our answer: read-only integration, zero remote-control capability, network-segmented deployment.

AI cost

  • Anomaly detection: small time-series model, ~24 values/day per substation.
  • The LLM only drafts the human-audit report.
  • ≈ €0.05–0.20 per user/month — trivially covered by the subscription.
